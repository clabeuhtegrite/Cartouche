# Launcher

The interface players see: a console-style catalogue of every game Cartouche supports, with the player's own games first. Its SwiftUI sources are in [`Sources/`](Sources/). `app/scripts/prepare.sh` adds them to LiveContainer's SwiftUI sources, [`livecontainer/0003`](../app/patches/livecontainer/0003-open-on-cartouches-games-with-its-system-page.patch) opens the app on them, and [`livecontainer/0006`](../app/patches/livecontainer/0006-show-none-of-livecontainers-own-pages.patch) leaves them alone on screen. Its bridge to the built-in SideStore, [`SideStoreSupport/`](SideStoreSupport/), joins LiveContainer's SideStoreSupport framework the same way.

## Screens

Every game appears as the front of its game case: its official cover from IGDB, whole, in a rounded rectangle of a case's proportions, which the iPhone loads itself and keeps in its caches ([`CartoucheCovers.swift`](Sources/CartoucheCovers.swift)). A cover of another shape stands over a blurred fill of itself, never cropped. For a game IGDB has no cover for, and until the cover has loaded, the tile shows the icon of the player's installed copy, or art we design from the game's name, genre and platform ([`CartoucheTiles.swift`](Sources/CartoucheTiles.swift)). The cartridge we drew around each cover until 0.9 stays in Cartouche's icon and name only ([decision 31](../docs/decisions.md)). A game's sheet stands on the game's official artwork, when IGDB has one. The repository and the app carry none of these images, only IGDB's IDs ([catalog/README.md](../catalog/README.md)).

- **Home, the console's front page** ([`CartoucheHomeView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheHomeView.swift)), on a dark stage that takes the colours of the game in focus:
  - **My library,** the one shelf: the player's games, installed or in their games folder, then an **Import** tile and **All games**. The games nxnative plays on an iPhone that the player has not imported are in All games, under their filter, not on the home ([decision 34](../docs/decisions.md)).
  - The game in focus: its name, platform, genre and status on this iPhone, **Play** for the player's own game or **Import my copy** for another, **Update** when the player's games folder holds a newer build of their game, and **Details**, its sheet. Such a game's cover shows a badge. The game paused behind the console has **Resume** and **Quit** instead, and a pause badge on its cover; the focus goes back to it whenever the console comes back over it. A line under the status says what is left to do, such as a setup to finish, and stays away when the game is ready to play.
  - A dock to All games, Import and System, at the bottom when the view is taller than wide and in the top bar when it is wider, and at the top the controller when one is connected and, in the last three days, the days left before the signature expires. The shelf's covers take the height the view has left, up to about two covers and a third across, so that the dock never covers them, on an iPhone held either way or a Duo folded or not; the shelf and the game in focus stand in the middle of the room they leave, as a console's do.
  - Touch and a game controller both drive it ([`CartoucheGamepad.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGamepad.swift)). A tap puts a game in focus, a second tap opens its sheet, and a long press on one of the player's games offers **Play**, **Update from** the games folder when it holds a newer build, **Details** and **Uninstall**, or **Resume**, **Quit** and **Details** for the game paused behind the console. With a controller, the D-pad moves, A does the main action, X opens the sheet, Y shows every game and Menu the system page; each button of the home and of the dock shows the controller's button for it, as the controller draws it.
- **All games** ([`CartoucheLibraryView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheLibraryView.swift)): every game of nxnative's list ([`catalog/nxnative.json`](../catalog/nxnative.json)), the player's first, then those nxnative plays on the iPhone, each cover with its name and status under it, filtered by All, Mine, iPhone, Mac, Bring-up or Switch 2, and searched by name or title ID. Mac lists the games nxnative plays on the iPhone too, since it plays them on the Mac as well. A game in the player's games folder that is not installed shows with **Install from** that place. With a controller, L and R change the filter and B goes back.
- **Game sheet:** at the top, **Play** and **Game settings** for the player's own game, or **Resume**, **Game settings** and **Quit** while it is paused behind the console, **Install from** the games folder for a game there, or **Import my copy**, and **Update from** the games folder for the player's game when it holds a newer build, with the build on this iPhone and the newer one; then what the game needs against what this iPhone has (memory limit measured on the phone, iOS version, GPU features), folded into one line when the iPhone has it all; the copies of its saves; for a game that starts in Cartouche's place for its memory, why, and **Try behind Cartouche again**; **Copy to** the games folder, and **Uninstall** last, in red, but for the game that runs; testers' results; and, folded under **Technical details**, the recommended settings, what nxnative says of the game (where it plays it, the engine, the game version and SDK of its profile, the title ID and the other regions) and the build installed.
- **Game settings** ([`CartoucheGameSettings.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGameSettings.swift)): the settings nxnative's own screen shows before the game, in Cartouche's language, with the cloud saves folder and **Reset to tested defaults**. What a setting does is behind an info button beside its name, and the categories share the width when their names fit it.
- **Import:** an IPA the player built with nxnative from their own copy, from Files, iCloud Drive, a USB-C drive or a network share, or from the player's games folder.
- **System,** over the console: signature days left and renewal, the player's storage, updates, the memory probe, whether games run behind the console, the game measurements Cartouche sends, and **Report a problem**. **OK** or the controller's Menu button closes it. It and every page under it, the setup and the sign-in stand on the console's dark stage, whatever the iPhone's appearance.

The catalogue shows what Cartouche can run, never where to get a game: there is no download button and no hint of one, and a game the player does not have offers only **Import my copy**, which opens Files.

## What it does

The launcher, on iOS 17 or later; an older iOS shows that Cartouche needs iOS 17. It shows none of LiveContainer's or SideStore's pages ([decision 24](../docs/decisions.md)): no tab bar, the console fills the window, and the System page opens over it from the dock or the controller's Menu button ([`CartoucheRootView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheRootView.swift)). LiveContainer's app list, settings, Sources and Tweaks are gone, as are the **Open SideStore** button, the app's page in the Settings app and LiveContainer's share sheet extensions (`app/scripts/package.sh`): Cartouche downloads no app, games need no tweak, and Cartouche drives LiveContainer and the built-in SideStore itself. A link opened with Cartouche goes to Cartouche or nowhere: a pairing file to the setup, an IPA to the installer; links to install an app from an address, open a web page, launch an app or take a certificate are dropped. Until Cartouche signs the player in on a screen of its own, the setup still opens the built-in SideStore's onboarding for the Apple ID.

- **Games.** The sheets of [`catalog/`](../catalog/) and nxnative's list of games as of the build, which `app/scripts/package.sh` puts in the app (`CartoucheCatalog/`): a new version of Cartouche brings new ones. The games' covers come with them, as IGDB's IDs, and Cartouche reads a newer list of them every day from the branch `live-covers` ([catalog/README.md](../catalog/README.md)), when it opens and when it comes back, once the one it read is six hours old ([`CartoucheCoverList.swift`](Sources/CartoucheCoverList.swift)): it has the covers of the games nxnative added since the version, and of those IGDB had none for yet. A game of the player's that nxnative's list in the app does not know shows its cover by the title ID of its copy.
  - The player's games are found among LiveContainer's apps by the bundle identifier nxnative gives its iOS apps, `org.nxnative.<title ID>`, or else by the `DMLanguage` key it writes into their `Info.plist`. A game is matched to its sheet by title ID, or by name when the build had another bundle identifier. A game without a sheet still shows, as *No sheet yet*, and starts.
  - **Import my copy** opens Files, and Cartouche installs the IPA itself, with LiveContainer's own steps ([`CartoucheGameActions.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGameActions.swift)): it reads the IPA where it is, as a game's IPA is too large to copy first, unpacks and signs it over the console with the progress, then opens the game's sheet, Play first. Cartouche keeps the screen awake meanwhile, and first checks that the iPhone has room for the unpacked game. An IPA opened with Cartouche from another app, AirDrop or Files is installed the same way. An IPA of a game already installed replaces it in place, with its settings and saves, as LiveContainer's Replace does.
  - **Play** starts the game behind the console, with iOS 18 or later ([decision 38](../docs/decisions.md), [`CartoucheGameHost.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGameHost.swift)): in LiveContainer's LiveProcess extension, a process of its own, whose scene Cartouche shows full screen in a window over the console, as LiveContainer's multitasking windows show theirs. A Home button at the bottom of the game's screen, and the Home button of a controller, which then comes to Cartouche rather than to iOS, bring the console back: the game's scene goes to the background, where nxnative pauses the game, and the controller's other buttons go back to the console. **Resume**, or the controller's Home button again, brings the game back where it was; **Quit** ends it, once the player has said so, and so does Play on another game, which then starts. Cartouche's own update waits until the game has ended. Cartouche's part of the game's process ([`LiveProcessSupport/CartoucheGuest.m`](LiveProcessSupport/CartoucheGuest.m), started by [`livecontainer/0008`](../app/patches/README.md)) passes the presses of the controller's Home button on while the game has the controller, and writes the game's memory every two seconds. A game starts in Cartouche's place instead, which opens again as the game, as LiveContainer's Run does, when the player turns **Keep Cartouche open behind the game** off on the System page, when LiveContainer runs it only there (classic mode, JIT or 32-bit), or when it needs more memory than it has behind the console: the limit iOS gave the game's process is read as the game starts, and a game that needs more, by its sheet, starts again in Cartouche's place at once; one that iOS stopped behind the console close to its limit starts there from then on, and its sheet offers to try again. A game that needs signing again is signed first, with the progress. nxnative shows its own settings screen before a game unless the game's `Documents/env.txt` holds `DM_LAUNCHER=0`: Play writes that line, and the game starts at once with the choices saved in its preferences.
  - **Game settings** reads the game's settings list, `dm/launcher.json`, which nxnative's build writes from its `settings.tsv`, and shows each setting as nxnative's screen does: sections, one at a time, picked on a row of buttons or with L and R on a controller, the cloud saves last; sub-settings that show only while the setting above them is on, the render scale's size in pixels, notes. Names, notes and options are translated into French; a text Cartouche does not know yet shows as nxnative wrote it, and the game's languages take their names from iOS. Each choice is saved at once where the game reads it: the game's own preferences (`launcher.<variable>`, only a choice that differs from the game's default; `cloudSavesBookmark` for the cloud saves folder), in its data folder, through cfprefsd, as LiveContainer's own Preferences page for a data folder opens them. A setting an env file fixes shows, greyed out. **Start on nxnative's screen** starts the game on its settings screen, without the `DM_LAUNCHER=0` line, for that one start.
  - **Uninstall** asks whether the saves stay. Kept, the game's data folder stays with them, without what the game makes again at each start, and the game's next import takes it back; erased, it goes with the game's keychain entries, as LiveContainer deletes them. With a games folder set up, it also offers to copy the game there first.
- **Storage,** the player's own, off the iPhone ([decision 23](../docs/decisions.md), [`CartoucheStorage.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorage.swift), [`CartoucheStorageView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageView.swift)), on the System page:
  - *Places:* a shared folder of the player's Mac, PC or NAS, over SMB 2 or 3 ([SMBClient](https://github.com/kishikawakatsumi/SMBClient), pinned in `app/SMBClient` and linked by [`livecontainer/0005`](../app/patches/livecontainer/0005-link-the-smbclient-package.patch)), with the account of that computer or as a guest; the servers on the local network show by themselves (Bonjour, `_smb._tcp`), after iOS asks to let Cartouche reach it. Or a folder in Files, which iOS lets Cartouche keep. Each password stays in this iPhone's keychain, never in the preferences. There is no address to paste for a download, no link to follow and no public bucket: only the player's places, with their own account.
  - *Games folder:* the library lists each IPA there, and the folders one level down ([`CartoucheStoredGames.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStoredGames.swift)). An IPA goes to its game by the game's title ID, with nothing for the player to set. One Cartouche or Cartouche Builder wrote is known by its name, `<game> [<title ID>].ipa`; any other by its `Info.plist`, read where the IPA sits, in a few reads even for several gigabytes, and remembered: the bundle identifier nxnative gives the game, `org.nxnative.<title ID>`, else its display name. Failing those, by its file name ([`CartoucheCatalog.swift`](Sources/CartoucheCatalog.swift), `titleId(inFileName:)`): a title ID the catalogue knows anywhere in it, the name of a game, or the name of a game at its start, as in `MarioKartWorld-nxnative-v2.ipa`, the longest such name, of six letters and digits at least. An IPA iCloud Drive keeps online only is known by its file name, since reading it would download it all; Cartouche reads it at the next scan once it is downloaded, unless its name said what it is. An IPA none of these tell is not listed. Its `Info.plist`, read for every IPA, also says which build of the game it is ([`CartoucheGameBuild.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGameBuild.swift)): the game's version, the nxnative commit and shader chain nxnative writes there (`DMCommit`, `DMChain`), and when the IPA was built, the time of its `Info.plist` in the zip. An IPA is a newer build of an installed game when it is a later version of the game, or the same version built later from another nxnative commit or from changed files, such as one Cartouche Builder made after a change to nxnative ([builder/README.md](../builder/README.md)); one that names no commit is newer only by its version, so that a copy of the installed game written there later is never offered over it. The installed game's build is the one its IPA said when Cartouche installed it, kept in `Application Support/Cartouche/InstalledBuilds.json`, since the install changes the time of its `Info.plist`; for a game installed before 0.9.0, its install date stands for it, since it was built before. The console then offers **Update** for that game, which installs the newer IPA over it as **Install from** does: its settings and saves stay. Of several IPAs of one game, the library takes the newest. **Install from** unpacks the IPA as it arrives ([`CartoucheZip.swift`](Sources/CartoucheZip.swift)), so the iPhone never holds the IPA and the game at once, after checking that the iPhone has room. A connection that drops on the way, when the Wi-Fi cuts out, the NAS restarts or iOS takes the connection of a Cartouche away from the screen, is made again for three minutes, and the install reads on from where it stopped, saying so meanwhile ([`CartoucheResumingSource.swift`](Sources/CartoucheResumingSource.swift)); an SMB request that gets no answer for a minute cuts its connection, which is then made again the same way, and a goodbye to a server that no longer answers waits five seconds at most. Installs and copies also keep going for the half-minute iOS gives an app that leaves the screen. **Copy to** writes an installed game there as an IPA, after checking that the place has room. A games folder the player's account can only read still fills the library, but offers no **Copy to**, and **Uninstall** no copy first.
  - *Saves folder:* when a game behind the console ends, and, since a game in Cartouche's place is Cartouche itself while it plays, when Cartouche opens again after a game and whenever it comes back to the front, each game whose saves changed gets a copy there, but for the game that runs ([`CartoucheSaveBackups.swift`](Sources/CartoucheSaveBackups.swift)): a zip of the game's `Documents/save` and `Documents/save_device`, the folders nxnative keeps its saves in, as `<game> [<title ID>]/<date>.zip`, the ten newest kept. **Play** waits for the copies under way. The game's sheet lists the copies and puts one back, after copying the saves it replaces; nxnative's working copy, `save.work`, goes, for the game to make it again. It has to be a folder the player's account can write to: one it can only read is not offered.
  - *Read-only places:* whether the account can write is asked, never tried: the permissions of a shared folder come with the connection to it (SMB's `MaximalAccess`), and a folder's are asked by opening it to add a file and a folder in it, which adds neither ([`CartoucheStorageSMB.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageSMB.swift), `canWrite`); a folder in Files answers as its file system says. Cartouche asks when a server is saved, when the player looks through its folders, when it reads the games folder and before each copy, and keeps the answers, so that a shared folder the account can only read shows *read-only* and cannot hold the saves.
  - *S3:* a private bucket of the player's, on Amazon S3 or on a server of their own such as MinIO on a NAS, with an access key of their account ([`CartoucheStorageS3.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageS3.swift)). Every request is signed with the player's keys (AWS Signature Version 4, [`CartoucheS3.swift`](Sources/CartoucheS3.swift), tested against AWS's own examples), and none goes without them: there is no way to reach a public bucket. The address goes over HTTPS unless the player types `http://` for a server on their network; the region, when left empty, is the one the address names, else `us-east-1`, and Cartouche signs for the bucket's own when S3 says it is another. Amazon's buckets are named in the host, others in the path. "Folders" are keys up to a `/`, as S3 consoles show them. A game reads with ranged requests, 4 MiB at least at a time; a copy goes in one request up to 16 MiB, in parts otherwise, which S3 assembles only at the end, so that the file there stays until then. S3 cannot be asked whether keys may write: Cartouche writes a hidden file and removes it. The bucket is saved only once it lists for the keys.
  - *FTP:* a server of the player's network, such as their NAS's, with their account on it ([`CartoucheStorageFTP.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageFTP.swift)): Cartouche's own client on Network.framework, with no dependency. Data connections are passive (EPSV, else PASV), always to the server Cartouche signed in to, whatever address PASV names. Folders are listed with MLSD when the server has it, as its FEAT says by naming MLST (RFC 3659), else LIST as Unix and Windows servers write it ([`CartoucheFTP.swift`](Sources/CartoucheFTP.swift)). Listing and folders share one control connection, a command at a time; each file read or written gets one of its own. A game reads from an offset (REST, then RETR), 4 MiB at least at a time; a copy is written as `<name>.partial` and renamed once finished. FTP sends the password unencrypted, so the form says it is for the player's own network; the server is saved only once the account gets in, and an empty user name is refused.
  - *SFTP:* a server of the player's, such as their NAS's or their Mac's, with their account on it ([`CartoucheStorageSFTP.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageSFTP.swift)): the SSH connection is Apple's swift-nio-ssh, linked by [`0007`](../app/patches/livecontainer/0007-link-the-swift-nio-ssh-package.patch), and SFTP version 3 on it is Cartouche's own ([`CartoucheSFTP.swift`](Sources/CartoucheSFTP.swift)). The server's key (ed25519 or ECDSA, as swift-nio-ssh takes) is kept with the server when the player saves it, its fingerprint shown in the form; at each connection a server showing another key is refused before the password is sent, until the player saves the server again. Sign-in is by password only. Paths are from the folder the server opens the account in. Reads and writes go in 32 KiB requests, sixteen at a time on one channel; a game reads 4 MiB at least at a time, and a copy is written as `<name>.partial`, then put in place of the file there, if any. Whether the account can write is asked by making an empty hidden file and removing it. Builds without the package, such as the gallery, leave SFTP out.
- **How a game fits this iPhone,** from its sheet and what the launcher measures here:
  - *Not supported* when iOS is older than the game needs, or the GPU cannot read BC-compressed textures;
  - *Not recommended* when the game needs Increased Memory Limit and Cartouche does not have it, when its measured peak is above the limit measured here, or when the latest result on this model is *does not run*;
  - *Runs on this iPhone* when the latest result on this model is playable;
  - *Not tried on this iPhone yet* otherwise. On the player's own game, the badge reads *Ready* when nothing stands in the way.
- **System.**
  - The memory limit iOS sets for Cartouche, which the games that start in its place share, and the one it set for the last game behind the console, measured as in [`devices.json`](../catalog/devices.json): footprint plus `os_proc_available_memory()`. Whether the signature carries Increased Memory Limit, and what testers measured on this model.
  - When the signature expires, the days left, and when it was last renewed. SideStore renews an app by installing a new provisioning profile and leaves the `embedded.mobileprovision` inside the app as its last install wrote it, so the launcher takes the later of that date and the one the built-in SideStore records for its widget after each renewal (`widget_data.json` in the app group).
  - **Renew now** renews without leaving Cartouche, with LocalDevVPN connected, and shows the progress. It runs the same refresh as the **Refresh All Apps** action that LiveContainer gives Shortcuts: the built-in SideStore starts in LiveProcess and renews every app it manages, Cartouche first. The launcher cannot import SideStoreSupport, so [`CartoucheRefresh`](SideStoreSupport/CartoucheRefresh.swift) joins that framework and the launcher finds it by its Objective-C name.
  - **Renew every night** explains the automation that renews Cartouche with no computer: in Shortcuts, a *Time of Day* automation, daily and *Run Immediately*, that runs Cartouche's *Refresh All Apps*, with LocalDevVPN left connected. iOS lets no app create the automation itself.
  - **Expiry alerts,** once the player allows them, 24 hours and 6 hours before the signature expires, and when it has. SideStore schedules its own after each renewal, in English and about "SideStore", under the same identifiers (`sidestore-expiration-warning.24h`, `.6h`, `.0h`), so each replaces the other: after a renewal in the background, SideStore's take the new date, and Cartouche writes its own again whenever it comes to the foreground. A separate warning two days ahead would keep the old date after a renewal in the background, so there is none. [`app/tests/test_renewal.py`](../app/tests/test_renewal.py) checks these identifiers, and the bridge's names, against the pinned sources.
  - **Updates** ([`CartoucheUpdates.swift`](Sources/CartoucheUpdates.swift)), without a computer and without showing SideStore. The launcher reads Cartouche's source, the one the built-in SideStore reads ([`app/sidestore-source.json`](../app/sidestore-source.json), whose address `app/scripts/package.sh` writes into the Info.plist as `CartoucheSourceURL`), when Cartouche opens and, at most once an hour, when it comes back, and picks the newest version for this iOS as SideStore does ([`CartoucheRelease.swift`](Sources/CartoucheRelease.swift)). A higher version than Cartouche's own is offered over the console, once Cartouche is set up, with **Update** and **Later**, which puts it off for a day ([`CartoucheUpdateView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheUpdateView.swift)); the System page always shows it, and **Check for an update** reads the source again. **Update** has the built-in SideStore run its *Update Cartouche* action ([`app/patches/sidestore/0010`](../app/patches/README.md)) in LiveProcess, as **Renew now** does, through [`CartoucheUpdate`](SideStoreSupport/CartoucheUpdate.swift): it reads the source again, downloads the IPA, checks its size and SHA-256, signs it and installs it over Cartouche, with LocalDevVPN connected. iOS replaces an app only once it has left the screen, and SideStore, in LiveProcess, cannot leave it: it posts the Darwin notification `<bundle identifier>.SelfReinstall` as it starts the install, and Cartouche goes to the home screen by itself three seconds later. iOS closes it and installs the new version; the games and their data stay. When the player opens Cartouche again, it says whether the version it was installing is the one running.
  - This iPhone: model, chip and memory from `devices.json`, iOS, and the GPU's BC support. Cartouche's version with the commit it was built from, the LiveContainer and SideStore it is based on, and how many sheets it carries ([app/README.md](../app/README.md#version)).
  - **Game measurements** ([`CartoucheTelemetry.swift`](Sources/CartoucheTelemetry.swift), [`CartoucheTelemetryView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheTelemetryView.swift)), sent unless the player turns them off ([decision 35](../docs/decisions.md), [docs/privacy.md](../docs/privacy.md)). A game runs as Cartouche itself, so when Cartouche opens or comes back, and when a game it runs beside itself ends, it reads the log nxnative wrote for each session: `Documents/log.txt` of the game's data folder, which nxnative renames `log.prev.txt` at the game's next start; each is read once, known by its folder and when it was created, and not while its game runs. [`CartoucheGameLog.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGameLog.swift) takes only numbers out of it, line by line, and a line it does not know leaves its value out, so that a change to nxnative's lines makes the report emptier, never wrong. The report of the session, with the game, its build and its settings, and Cartouche's events, each start, installs, updates, renewals and errors by their codes ([`CartoucheTelemetryReport.swift`](Sources/CartoucheTelemetryReport.swift)), wait on the iPhone, 300 at most and for 30 days, and go to the telemetry server ([`telemetry/`](../telemetry/README.md)) 25 at a time, again later while it is away. The page shows the switch, what is sent and what never is, the last report as it left, how many went and wait, **Erase what this iPhone sent** and the privacy page. The server's address is `CARTOUCHE_TELEMETRY_URL` in [`app/config.env`](../app/config.env), which `app/scripts/package.sh` writes into the Info.plist as `CartoucheTelemetryURL`: a build without one sends nothing.
  - **Report a problem** ([`CartoucheReportView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheReportView.swift)): the player writes what happened and sees all the report holds before anything leaves the iPhone. It is the report Cartouche writes of an error ([`CartoucheReport.swift`](Sources/CartoucheReport.swift)), with what the player wrote as its message: Cartouche and this iPhone, then its last steps; then the reports of its last errors, the five newest of the week. All of it, what the player wrote included, loses servers' names and addresses, accounts and paths, as an error's report does, and Cartouche never puts a password in it. Last comes this iPhone's memory limit, as an entry of [`devices.json`](../catalog/devices.json) ([`CartoucheProblemReport.swift`](Sources/CartoucheProblemReport.swift)), so that a tester's report adds their iPhone to it ([`add_device.py`](../catalog/tools/add_device.py)); the page says so when the list does not have the model yet. The player sends it themselves: with **Send to Cartouche**, when Cartouche has its server's address, to the project's own server, which files it as an issue of the project's private repository ([`telemetry/`](../telemetry/README.md#problems)), the same report under the same form's fields, in at most 60,000 bytes; with the share sheet, to whoever helps them, by Messages, Mail or AirDrop; by copying it; or with **Report it on GitHub**, which opens the repository's [bug report form](../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/bug_report.yml) with its fields filled in from the address, for them to check and post with their GitHub account; what they wrote goes in the form's own field. Reports there are public, and the page says so. GitHub refuses an address past about 8 KB, so the form's logs lose their last lines first, the oldest errors, which the share sheet keeps. A report leaves the iPhone only when the player sends it ([decision 36](../docs/decisions.md)).
- **Your first game,** the guide of a player new to Cartouche ([`CartoucheGuideView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGuideView.swift), [`CartoucheGuide.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGuide.swift)): five steps from a new install to a game on the iPhone, LocalDevVPN, the Apple ID, the game built on a Mac, the games folder, then the game installed, each ticked from what Cartouche sees, in any order. The step left first is open with its action, one Cartouche already has; a later one opens with a tap. LocalDevVPN counts as connected by its tunnel, `utun<n>` with an address in 10.7.0.0/16, as the built-in SideStore's onboarding reads it, without connecting to anything ([`CartoucheVPN.swift`](Sources/CartoucheVPN.swift)); **Connect LocalDevVPN** has it connect and open Cartouche again by Cartouche's own URL scheme, and the step ticks itself. The Mac's step says what the Mac needs and sends the Mac [docs/getting-started.md](../docs/getting-started.md#2-build-your-game-on-your-mac), by AirDrop or Messages, whose one command installs Cartouche Builder ([`builder/install.sh`](../builder/install.sh)). The guide opens by itself once, the first time the console shows before the player has signed in, then from a card at the top of the home, in the setup banner's place, until the player's first game is installed; from then on it shows no more, and the setup banner says what may be missing.
- **Setup,** at the top of the console and the System page until it is done, and wherever **Play** would fail without a certificate ([`CartoucheSetup.swift`](Sources/CartoucheSetup.swift)):<a id="setup"></a>
  1. *Pairing file:* only a computer makes it. The [Cartouche Installer](../installer/README.md) puts it there when it installs Cartouche, so the step is done when Cartouche first opens. iloader's **Export** saves one too, and the player drops it on Cartouche in Apple Devices, iTunes or the Finder, which list Cartouche among the apps that share files and put the file at the top of its Documents. Cartouche takes a file there, or in `Documents/Inbox`, once it reads as a pairing file, and moves it into the built-in SideStore's Documents as `ALTPairingFile.mobiledevicepairing`, where a LiveContainer+SideStore's SideStore reads it; a file dropped later replaces it. The step looks every two seconds while there is none, and whenever Cartouche comes to the front, so it ticks itself while the player is still at the computer. A file can also be opened with Cartouche from AirDrop, Mail or Files ([`app/patches/livecontainer/0004`](../app/patches/livecontainer/0004-hand-a-pairing-file-opened-from-another-app-to-cartouches-setup.patch)), which shows the setup, or chosen here; Cartouche checks it and copies it to the same place. iloader's **Place In All Apps** does not list Cartouche ([app/README.md](../app/README.md#identity)).
  2. *Apple ID:* **Sign In** opens Cartouche's own sign-in screen ([`CartoucheSignInView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheSignInView.swift)): the Apple ID and its password, then the verification code Apple sends to the player's devices or by text message, with LocalDevVPN connected. The built-in SideStore signs in with them without showing itself, in LiveProcess, as **Renew now** does: Cartouche leaves the Apple ID and password in the keychain they share, under SideStore's service, and runs SideStore's *Sign In to Cartouche* action ([`app/patches/sidestore/0011`](../app/patches/README.md)) through [`CartoucheSignIn`](SideStoreSupport/CartoucheSignIn.swift). SideStore reads them once and keeps them in its own items, as after any sign-in; when Apple asks for a code, it leaves a request in the same keychain, which Cartouche shows, and reads Cartouche's answer ([`CartoucheAccount.swift`](Sources/CartoucheAccount.swift), [`CartoucheSignInMessage.swift`](Sources/CartoucheSignInMessage.swift), checked by [`app/tests/test_sign_in.py`](../app/tests/test_sign_in.py)). SideStore signs in through the SideStore (New) anisette server, registers the iPhone and takes the certificate the installer, or iloader, wrote into it ([app/README.md](../app/README.md#identity)); an error, such as an account Apple asks to review, shows on the screen, with the page to open. The step reads as done once SideStore's keychain holds the Apple ID, and Cartouche counts as set up, for renewals and updates, once it does. With an Apple ID but no certificate, **Sign in again** is offered.
  3. *Certificate:* Cartouche reads SideStore's certificate from their shared keychain (service: Cartouche's bundle identifier, `CartoucheBundleIdentifier` in the Info.plist) and hands it to LiveContainer, as LiveContainer's *Import Certificate from SideStore* does after a confirmation. It does so whenever it comes to the front, and again when SideStore's certificate changes; one whose key does not open is left aside. Cartouche then checks, once per certificate and launch, that iOS runs what LiveContainer signs with it, as LiveContainer's own test of its setup does: a certificate Apple has revoked, as the installer does with an old one when the account has too many, would make every game fail with *the app is signed with the latest certificate but its signature is invalid*. Cartouche then has SideStore sign in again by itself, with the Apple ID and password it keeps, and SideStore takes the certificate the installer wrote into Cartouche; the sign-in screen opens only should Apple ask for a code, and **Sign in again** stays offered should it fail. Until the certificate passes, Cartouche offers no update, since an update signed with a revoked certificate would not open.
  - LocalDevVPN, for renewals, with a link to the App Store.
- **Reports** ([`CartoucheReport.swift`](Sources/CartoucheReport.swift), [`CartoucheReporting.swift`](Sources/CartoucheReporting.swift)): under every error Cartouche shows, **Copy the report** puts on the pasteboard what the player can paste wherever they ask for help. It says what failed and the message the player read; the error's domain and code, those of the errors under it, and what an SMB, S3, FTP or SFTP error holds (its NTSTATUS, HTTP status and S3 code, FTP reply or SFTP status); the step Cartouche was at and how far it had gone, with that step's sizes and the game's build; Cartouche's version and commit, the LiveContainer and SideStore it is based on, iOS and its build, the model, its memory limit and free space, how far the setup is and which kinds of storage the player uses; then the last 40 lines of Cartouche's journal. The journal, the last 200 lines in `Application Support/Cartouche/Journal.txt`, notes each start of Cartouche, each step with a game and every quarter of the way, renewals, updates, sign-ins, saves copies and every problem; the last ten reports stay in `Reports.json` beside it. Neither ever holds a password or a key, which Cartouche never puts in a message. A report's text, the journal and the reports kept also take out what the player's settings name, as `<server>`, `<name>`, `<account>` or `<path>`: their servers' addresses, the names of their places, shared folders and buckets, the paths of their folders, their user names and access key IDs, their Apple ID and their team's name, and what they typed in a form before it was saved; and anything shaped like an address, a URL, an IPv4 or IPv6 address, a `.local` name or an e-mail address, or like the folder of an iPhone app ([`CartoucheReportTests.swift`](Tests/CartoucheCoreTests/CartoucheReportTests.swift)). In an alert, **Copy the report** closes it and says so at the top of the console; with a controller, Y copies it, and A or B closes the alert. Each report is also posted as `CartoucheErrorReported`, with where it went wrong as a key that stays the same in every language (`install`, `export`, `start`, `uninstall`, `update`, `renewal`, `pairing`, `signIn`, `storage`, `saves`, `cloud`). ## Every iPhone, the iPhone Duo included

The launcher lays itself out from the space it is given, never from the iPhone model, as Apple asks of apps for the iPhone Duo ([Preparing your app for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/preparing-your-app-for-iphone-duo)). One design then serves every iPhone, both displays of the Duo and the iPad. The model matters only to the game sheets, for memory.

| Where | Width class | Home |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Any other iPhone, portrait | compact | One column: the player's games, then the game in focus, in the middle, and the dock. A game's sheet opens over the home. |
| Any iPhone, landscape | compact | The shelf beside the game in focus. |
| iPhone Duo folded: outer display, 5.4 in | compact | As on the other iPhones. iOS moves the bars to the side of the display. |
| iPhone Duo unfolded: inner display, 7.6 in | regular | The home on one side of the fold, its shelf only, and the sheet of the game in focus on the other: side by side when the display is wider than tall, the home above when it is taller. |
| iPad | regular | As on the unfolded Duo. |

- **Size classes and our own bounds, not the screen.** Layout follows `horizontalSizeClass` and the bounds of the scene or the containing view, never `UIScreen.main.bounds`, `userInterfaceIdiom` or the interface orientation.
- **System containers and bars.** `NavigationStack`, `toolbar(content:)` and the bars iOS provides, which it presents vertically on the side of the Duo's outer display, and for some views on the inner one. Every bar item has an icon and a title: iOS does not present a title-only item or a custom view vertically. The banner of the player's games extends under a vertical bar with `backgroundExtensionEffect()`.
- **Regular width.** A split `ArrangementView` (iOS 27.1) holds the home and the sheet of the game in focus, the same size each, and iOS places them around the fold: side by side, the home leading, when the display is wider than tall, the home above when it is taller, and it moves them as the Duo folds partly ([`CartoucheArrangement.swift`](Sources/CartoucheArrangement.swift)). It sits at the top level, not inside a split view, list or scroll view, as Apple advises. The home then leaves out the game in focus, whose sheet is beside it, and its shelf takes its whole width. The sheet follows the focus, by touch as with a controller, and opens the game's settings in its place; with a controller, B closes them and L and R change their section. Before iOS 27.1, the sheet opens as a card over the home.
- **Nothing in the fold or under a camera.** Views we draw ourselves read `reservedRegions(kind:options:layoutDirectionBehavior:)` and keep text and controls out of the fold while the Duo is partly folded, and out of the outer front camera, which always covers part of the outer display. System views move on their own.
- **Folding loses nothing.** Folding, unfolding or rotating keeps the selected game, the scroll position and any import in progress; imports run outside the views. The same `ArrangementView` stays on screen in every width, with no axis to split along in a compact one, so that the home is never built again: unfolding moves an open sheet beside the home, and folding brings open game settings back over it, in a sheet.
- **Built with Xcode 27.2.** Apple: an app built with Xcode 26 or earlier does not extend under the Duo's status bar and camera; on a Duo, 0.1 showed in a narrower band beside a black one that held the status bar. Since 0.2, CI builds Cartouche with Xcode 27.2 and the iOS 27.2 SDK on GitHub's `xcode-27` runners.
- **Tested** in the Duo simulator, by the gallery on every pull request, then on a Duo closed, open and partly folded, rotated in each pose, as Apple recommends.

### Games on the Duo

A game's picture and its touch controls are drawn by nxnative's iOS app, which runs inside Cartouche or, behind the console, in a process of its own whose scene fills a window of Cartouche's, and Cartouche does not patch games. Inside Cartouche, system libraries work to the SDK version Cartouche was built with, not the game's, unless LiveContainer's per-app *Spoof SDK Version* setting is on ([`Dyld.m`](https://github.com/LiveContainer/LiveContainer/blob/9108ae624ea001f7d7696d48407f890b48fa2653/LiveContainer/Tweaks/Dyld.m)): built with Xcode 27.2, Cartouche lets games use the whole display, the camera area included. nxnative's iOS app lays its picture and touch controls out again whenever its view changes size, from the view's bounds, its safe area and the fold (`UIHingeInteraction`, iOS 27.1; `dm/ios/Sources/DmLayout.h` in nxnative): the controls over the picture when the view is wider than tall, unless the Duo is partly open over a horizontal fold, and otherwise the picture above the fold and the controls below. Each layout sizes the picture's drawable to the view, at the display's scale, and lets go of the buttons held, so that a player who folds the Duo mid-race presses them again. Still to check on a Duo: folding or unfolding mid-game neither restarts the game nor pushes it over its memory limit. Behind the console, the game's scene takes the bounds of Cartouche's window and follows them as the Duo folds or turns, and the game's process records each size it was given with its footprint (`sizes` in `Cartouche/GameMemory.plist`); still to check there: the fold as `UIHingeInteraction` reports it inside the game's scene, the reserved regions of the outer camera and of the fold, and the Home button at the bottom of the game's screen, which the views Cartouche draws over the game place by the safe area only.
