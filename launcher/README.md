# Launcher

The interface players see: a console-style catalogue of every game Cartouche supports, with the player's own games first. Its SwiftUI sources are in [`Sources/`](Sources/). `app/scripts/prepare.sh` adds them to LiveContainer's SwiftUI sources, [`livecontainer/0003`](../app/patches/livecontainer/0003-open-on-cartouches-games-with-its-system-page.patch) opens the app on them, and [`livecontainer/0006`](../app/patches/livecontainer/0006-show-none-of-livecontainers-own-pages.patch) leaves them alone on screen. Its bridge to the built-in SideStore, [`SideStoreSupport/`](SideStoreSupport/), joins LiveContainer's SideStoreSupport framework the same way, and the play time counter, [`Guest/`](Guest/), joins LiveContainerShared, which every LiveContainer process loads, the games' included.

## Screens

Every game appears as the front of its game case: its official cover from IGDB, whole, in a rounded rectangle of a case's proportions, which the iPhone loads itself and keeps in its caches ([`CartoucheCovers.swift`](Sources/CartoucheCovers.swift)). A cover of another shape stands over a blurred fill of itself, never cropped. For a game IGDB has no cover for, and until the cover has loaded, the tile shows the icon of the player's installed copy, or art we design from the game's name, genre and platform ([`CartoucheTiles.swift`](Sources/CartoucheTiles.swift)). The cartridge we drew around each cover until 0.9 stays in Cartouche's icon and name only ([decision 31](../docs/decisions.md)). A game's sheet, and the home and All games for the game in focus, stand on the game's official artwork, faintly, when IGDB has one, else on its cover. The repository and the app carry none of these images, only IGDB's IDs ([catalog/README.md](../catalog/README.md)). A game of the player's that nxnative's list does not know, such as one built with a fork of nxnative, has its platform from its title ID, and its cover, artwork and genre from IGDB by its name, which Cartouche asks its server about ([`CartoucheGameLookup.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGameLookup.swift), [telemetry/README.md](../telemetry/README.md)). The player can give any game of theirs a name, a platform, a genre and a cover of their own, from Photos or Files, which go over Cartouche's and stay on the iPhone ([`CartouchePlayerInfo.swift`](Sources/CartouchePlayerInfo.swift)): the stage then stands on their cover.

- **Home, the console's front page** ([`CartoucheHomeView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheHomeView.swift)), on a dark stage that takes the colours of the game in focus:
  - **My library,** the one shelf: the player's games, installed or in their games folder, then an **Import** tile and **All games**. The games nxnative plays on an iPhone that the player has not imported are in All games, under their filter, not on the home ([decision 34](../docs/decisions.md)).
  - The game in focus: its name, platform, genre and status on this iPhone, the time the player has played it and the space it takes on this iPhone, or its IPA's size in the games folder, **Play** for the player's own game or **Import my copy** for another, **Update** when the player's games folder holds a newer build of their game, and **Details**, its sheet. Such a game's cover shows a badge. The game paused behind the console has **Resume** and **Quit** instead, and a pause badge on its cover; the focus goes back to it whenever the console comes back over it. A line under the status says what is left to do, such as a setup to finish, and stays away when the game is ready to play.
  - A dock to All games, Import and System, at the bottom when the view is taller than wide and in the top bar when it is wider, and at the top the controller when one is connected and, in the last three days, the days left before the signature expires. The shelf's covers take the height the view has left, up to about two covers and a third across, so that the dock never covers them, on an iPhone held either way or a Duo folded or not; the shelf and the game in focus stand in the middle of the room they leave, as a console's do.
  - Touch and a game controller both drive it ([`CartoucheGamepad.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGamepad.swift)). A tap puts a game in focus, a second tap opens its sheet, and a long press on one of the player's games offers **Play**, **Update from** the games folder when it holds a newer build, **Details** and **Uninstall**, or **Resume**, **Quit** and **Details** for the game paused behind the console. With a controller, the D-pad moves, A does the main action, X opens the sheet, Y shows every game and Menu the system page; each button of the home and of the dock shows the controller's button for it, as the controller draws it.
- **All games** ([`CartoucheLibraryView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheLibraryView.swift)): every game of nxnative's list ([`catalog/nxnative.json`](../catalog/nxnative.json)), the player's first, then those nxnative plays on the iPhone, each cover with its name and status under it, filtered by All, Mine, iPhone, Mac, Bring-up or Switch 2, and searched by name or title ID. Mac lists the games nxnative plays on the iPhone too, since it plays them on the Mac as well. A game in the player's games folder that is not installed shows with **Install from** that place. With a controller, L and R change the filter and B goes back.
- **Game sheet:** at the top, **Play** and **Game settings** for the player's own game, or **Resume**, **Game settings** and **Quit** while it is paused behind the console, **Install from** the games folder for a game there, or **Import my copy**, and **Update from** the games folder for the player's game when it holds a newer build, with the build on this iPhone and the newer one; then the game's description, the player's or IGDB's summary, its first four lines and **More** for the rest ([`CartoucheGameSummaryView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGameSummaryView.swift)): IGDB's, in English, the iPhone translates into Cartouche's language with Apple's Translation, from iOS 18, at once when it has both languages, else when the player taps **Translate**, which first offers to download them, with **Show the original**, and once: the translation is kept in Cartouche's caches for that summary and language, and shows as the sheet opens the next times ([`CartoucheTranslations.swift`](Sources/CartoucheTranslations.swift)); then the time the player has played it, with how many times and when last, the space it takes on this iPhone, the game and its data apart, and its IPA's size in the games folder; what the game needs against what this iPhone has (memory limit measured on the phone, iOS version, GPU features), folded into one line when the iPhone has it all; on an iPhone whose GPU nxnative refuses, the A16's and older, that the game stops there before its first image, for a game without a sheet too; the copies of its saves; **Edit the game's info** for the player's own game, with what IGDB has of a game nxnative's list does not know, or that it has nothing under that name yet ([`CartoucheGameInfoView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGameInfoView.swift)): its name, its platform and its genre, each **Automatic** or the player's, its description, IGDB's summary until the player changes it, and its cover, **Choose from Photos** or **Choose a file**, shown as the console will show it, back to Cartouche's with one tap, and **Save** brings the sheet back to its top, the name and cover in view; for a game that starts in Cartouche's place for its memory, or because games behind the console run without Increased Memory Limit here, why, and **Try behind Cartouche again**; **Copy to** the games folder, and **Uninstall** last, in red, but for the game that runs; testers' results; and, folded under **Technical details**, the recommended settings, what nxnative says of the game (where it plays it, the engine, the game version and SDK of its profile, the title ID and the other regions) and the build installed.
- **Game settings** ([`CartoucheGameSettings.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGameSettings.swift)): the settings nxnative's own screen shows before the game, in Cartouche's language, with the cloud saves folder and **Reset to tested defaults**. What a setting does is behind an info button beside its name, and the categories share the width when their names fit it.
- **Import:** an IPA the player built with nxnative from their own copy, from Files, iCloud Drive, a USB-C drive or a network share, or from the player's games folder. On an iPhone whose GPU cannot read BC-compressed textures, which nxnative refuses for every game, the console asks first, with **Install anyway** and **Cancel** ([`CartoucheInstallQuestions.swift`](Sources/CartoucheInstallQuestions.swift)): players with an iPhone 13 Pro or an iPhone 15 installed games that closed at once ([decision 67](../docs/decisions.md)).
- **System,** over the console: signature days left and renewal, the player's storage, updates, the memory probe, whether games run behind the console, the game measurements Cartouche sends, and **Report a problem**. **OK** or the controller's Menu button closes it. It and every page under it, the setup and the sign-in stand on the console's dark stage, whatever the iPhone's appearance.

The catalogue shows what Cartouche can run, never where to get a game: there is no download button and no hint of one, and a game the player does not have offers only **Import my copy**, which opens Files.

## What it does

The launcher, on iOS 17 or later; an older iOS shows that Cartouche needs iOS 17. It shows none of LiveContainer's or SideStore's pages ([decision 24](../docs/decisions.md)): no tab bar, the console fills the window, and the System page opens over it from the dock or the controller's Menu button ([`CartoucheRootView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheRootView.swift)). LiveContainer's app list, settings, Sources and Tweaks are gone, as are the **Open SideStore** button, the app's page in the Settings app and LiveContainer's share sheet extensions (`app/scripts/package.sh`): Cartouche downloads no app, games need no tweak, and Cartouche drives LiveContainer and the built-in SideStore itself. A link opened with Cartouche goes to Cartouche or nowhere: a pairing file to the setup, an IPA to the installer; links to install an app from an address, open a web page, launch an app or take a certificate are dropped. Until Cartouche signs the player in on a screen of its own, the setup still opens the built-in SideStore's onboarding for the Apple ID.

- **Games.** The sheets of [`catalog/`](../catalog/) and nxnative's list of games as of the build, which `app/scripts/package.sh` puts in the app (`CartoucheCatalog/`): a new version of Cartouche brings new ones. The games' covers come with them, as IGDB's IDs, and Cartouche reads a newer list of them every day from the branch `live-covers` ([catalog/README.md](../catalog/README.md)), when it opens and when it comes back, once the one it read is six hours old ([`CartoucheCoverList.swift`](Sources/CartoucheCoverList.swift)): it has the covers of the games nxnative added since the version, and of those IGDB had none for yet. A game of the player's that nxnative's list in the app does not know shows its cover by the title ID of its copy. A game nxnative has no profile for and IGDB no cover by its title ID is looked up by its name on Cartouche's server, which asks IGDB: Cartouche keeps the answer in Application Support and asks again a month later, a server that does not answer an hour later or when it says ([`CartoucheGameLookup.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGameLookup.swift)). nxnative's games whose profile says no genre take IGDB's from the catalog's covers ([catalog/README.md](../catalog/README.md)).
  - The player's games are found among LiveContainer's apps by the bundle identifier nxnative gives its iOS apps, `org.nxnative.<title ID>`, or else by the `DMLanguage` key it writes into their `Info.plist`. A game is matched to its sheet by title ID, or by name when the build had another bundle identifier. A game without a sheet still shows, as *No sheet yet*, and starts.
  - **Import my copy** opens the Files picker, one picker that UIKit shows over whatever is on screen, the home, a game's sheet or the guide ([`CartoucheImportPicker.swift`](Sources/CartoucheImportPicker.swift)): one that leaves the screen without an answer, or never comes, closes within a second, so that the console takes the controller again and the next tap shows a new one, and the controller's B closes it. Each picker is measured as `import.picker` ([telemetry/README.md](../telemetry/README.md)): how it closed, how long it took to show, and how long Cartouche's main thread, which passes the picker every touch, took to answer meanwhile. Cartouche then installs the IPA itself, with LiveContainer's own steps ([`CartoucheGameActions.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGameActions.swift)): it reads the IPA where it is, as a game's IPA is too large to copy first, unpacks and signs it over the console with the progress, then opens the game's sheet, Play first. Cartouche keeps the screen awake meanwhile, and first checks, over the console already and off the main thread, that it can read the IPA and that the iPhone has room for the unpacked game; from iOS 26, the player can leave it meanwhile, as during an install from the games folder (below). An IPA opened with Cartouche from another app, AirDrop or Files is installed the same way. An IPA of a game already installed replaces it in place, with its settings and saves, as LiveContainer's Replace does.
  - **Play** starts the game behind the console, with iOS 18 or later ([decision 38](../docs/decisions.md), [`CartoucheGameHost.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGameHost.swift)): in LiveContainer's LiveProcess extension, a process of its own, whose scene Cartouche shows full screen in a window over the console, as LiveContainer's multitasking windows show theirs. A Home button at the bottom of the game's screen, and the Home button of a controller, which then comes to Cartouche rather than to iOS, bring the console back: the game's scene goes to the background, where nxnative pauses the game, and the controller's other buttons go back to the console. **Resume**, or the controller's Home button again, brings the game back where it was; **Quit** ends it, once the player has said so, and so does Play on another game, which then starts. Cartouche's own update waits until the game has ended. Cartouche's part of the game's process ([`LiveProcessSupport/CartoucheGuest.m`](LiveProcessSupport/CartoucheGuest.m), started by [`livecontainer/0008`](../app/patches/README.md)) passes the presses of the controller's Home button on while the game has the controller, and writes the game's memory every two seconds. A game starts in Cartouche's place instead, which opens again as the game, as LiveContainer's Run does, when the player turns **Keep Cartouche open behind the game** off on the System page, when LiveContainer runs it only there (classic mode, JIT or 32-bit), or when it needs more memory than it has behind the console and Cartouche's place has more: the limit iOS gave the game's process is read as the game starts, and a game that needs more, by its sheet, starts again in Cartouche's place at once; one that iOS stopped behind the console close to its limit starts there from then on, and its sheet offers to try again. The game's process also says whether it has Increased Memory Limit: once one ran without it while Cartouche has more, every game but one whose peak fits there starts in Cartouche's place, and one that iOS stopped there before its first reading starts again in Cartouche's place at once, as Donkey Kong Bananza, stopped within two seconds with 3,376 MiB, would have, until another install of Cartouche or **Try behind Cartouche again** ([decision 67](../docs/decisions.md)). With Game Mode, Cartouche has more than the game's process, 8,192 MiB against 6,144 on an iPhone with 12 GB, so Mario Kart World, which needs Increased Memory Limit, starts in Cartouche's place ([docs/architecture.md](../docs/architecture.md#memory)). Should the game's process have as much, every game stays behind the console, and a game iOS stopped there for its memory is told that it would have no more in Cartouche's place; one that went to Cartouche's place before goes behind the console again. A game that needs signing again is signed first, with the progress. nxnative shows its own settings screen before a game unless the game's `Documents/env.txt` holds `DM_LAUNCHER=0`: Play writes that line, and the game starts at once with the choices saved in its preferences.
  - **Game settings** reads the game's settings list, `dm/launcher.json`, which nxnative's build writes from its `settings.tsv`, and shows each setting as nxnative's screen does: sections, one at a time, picked on a row of buttons or with L and R on a controller, the cloud saves last; sub-settings that show only while the setting above them is on, the render scale's size in pixels, notes. Names, notes and options are translated into French; a text Cartouche does not know yet shows as nxnative wrote it, and the game's languages take their names from iOS. Each choice is saved at once where the game reads it: the game's own preferences (`launcher.<variable>`, only a choice that differs from the game's default; `cloudSavesBookmark` for the cloud saves folder), in its data folder, through cfprefsd, as LiveContainer's own Preferences page for a data folder opens them. A setting an env file fixes shows, greyed out. **Start on nxnative's screen** starts the game on its settings screen, without the `DM_LAUNCHER=0` line, for that one start.
  - **Game language** follows the iPhone's ([`CartoucheGameLanguage.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGameLanguage.swift)), as a Switch gives a game its own language when the game has it. Before each start, Cartouche picks the first of the iPhone's languages, in the order of Settings › General › Language & Region, that the game lists, a language's other forms counting as that language, the iPhone's own first: American or British English, by the iPhone's region; French of France or Canada; Spanish of Spain or Latin America; Portuguese of Portugal or Brazil; Simplified or Traditional Chinese. A game that has none of them starts in the language it was built with; one whose files do not list its languages gets the first of the iPhone's that a Switch has, and may show another, as nxnative's note on the setting says. The language is saved where nxnative keeps the player's choice, `launcher.DM_LANG`, so that its own screen shows it too, and the game's preferences note that Cartouche chose it (`cartouche.languageSource`, `cartouche.iPhoneLanguage`). The setting's first option, **iPhone language**, says which language that is. A language the player picks, in Cartouche or on nxnative's screen, stays the game's until they pick **iPhone language** again or **Reset to tested defaults**, and so does one a game had saved before Cartouche first chose. A game of one language has nothing to choose, and an env file's `DM_LANG` still wins. Each start notes the game's language in the journal.
  - **Uninstall** asks whether the saves stay. Kept, the game's data folder stays with them, without what the game makes again at each start, and the game's next import takes it back; erased, it goes with the game's keychain entries, as LiveContainer deletes them. With a games folder set up, it also offers to copy the game there first.
- **Play time and size** ([`CartouchePlayTime.swift`](Sources/CartouchePlayTime.swift), [`CartoucheGameStats.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGameStats.swift)). A game counts its own play time, the time it is in front and active, with [`Guest/CartouchePlayTimeCounter.m`](Guest/CartouchePlayTimeCounter.m), which `app/scripts/prepare.sh` adds to LiveContainerShared, the framework every LiveContainer process loads first, a game's in Cartouche's process or in LiveProcess alike. It counts in nxnative's games only, listens to the game's life cycle and nothing else, and writes `Library/Cartouche/PlayTime.plist` in the game's data folder, the one place a game can write to in both: when the game leaves the front, and every 30 seconds while it is there, so that a game iOS stops loses half a minute at most. Cartouche reads the file whenever it comes back, and when a game it shows in its own window pauses or ends (`CartoucheGameDidPause`, `CartoucheGameDidEnd`), and keeps what it read by game and data folder in `Library/Application Support/Cartouche/PlayTime.json`: the time stays when a game is uninstalled with its saves, adds up with the time of its next install, and shows for a game that is only in the games folder. The space a game takes is measured as LiveContainer's storage page does, the allocated size of each file of the game's bundle and of its data folders, links not followed, in the background: again after another install of the game, once it was played, or a day later, and kept in the caches meanwhile. The games folder gives the size of each IPA.
- **Storage,** the player's own, off the iPhone ([decision 23](../docs/decisions.md), [`CartoucheStorage.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorage.swift), [`CartoucheStorageView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageView.swift)), on the System page:
  - *Places:* the player's Mac with Cartouche Builder (*Your Mac*, below), a shared folder of the player's Mac, PC or NAS, over SMB 2 or 3 ([SMBClient](https://github.com/kishikawakatsumi/SMBClient), pinned in `app/SMBClient` and linked by [`livecontainer/0005`](../app/patches/livecontainer/0005-link-the-smbclient-package.patch)), with the account of that computer or as a guest; the servers on the local network show by themselves (Bonjour, `_smb._tcp`), after iOS asks to let Cartouche reach it. Or the player's Google Drive (*Google Drive*, below), or a folder in Files, which iOS lets Cartouche keep. Each password stays in this iPhone's keychain, never in the preferences. The servers and the folders chosen, without their passwords, are kept in the keychain too: iOS removes an app's preferences with it, not its keychain items, so that Cartouche installed again finds them, and with them the games' saves. There is no address to paste for a download, no link to follow and no public bucket: only the player's places, with their own account.
  - *Games folder:* the library lists each IPA there, and the folders one level down ([`CartoucheStoredGames.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStoredGames.swift)). An IPA goes to its game by the game's title ID, with nothing for the player to set. One Cartouche or Cartouche Builder wrote is known by its name, `<game> [<title ID>].ipa`; any other by its `Info.plist`, read where the IPA sits, in a few reads even for several gigabytes, and remembered: the bundle identifier nxnative gives the game, `org.nxnative.<title ID>`, else the title ID nxnative writes there (`DMTitleID`) for a game built with another bundle identifier, else its display name. Failing those, by its file name ([`CartoucheCatalog.swift`](Sources/CartoucheCatalog.swift), `titleId(inFileName:)`): a title ID the catalogue knows anywhere in it, the name of a game, or the name of a game at its start, as in `MarioKartWorld-nxnative-v2.ipa`, the longest such name, of six letters and digits at least. An IPA iCloud Drive keeps online only is known by its file name, since reading it would download it all; Cartouche reads it at the next scan once it is downloaded, unless its name said what it is. An IPA none of these tell is not listed, unless what nxnative writes in the apps it builds (`DMLanguage`, `DMTitleID`, `DMCommit`), or the `romfs.id` of one built without its game files, says it is an nxnative game. Its `Info.plist`, read for every IPA, also says which build of the game it is ([`CartoucheGameBuild.swift`](Sources/CartoucheGameBuild.swift)): the game's version, the nxnative commit and shader chain nxnative writes there (`DMCommit`, `DMChain`), and when the IPA was built, the time of its `Info.plist` in the zip. An IPA is a newer build of an installed game when it is a later version of the game, or the same version built later from another nxnative commit or from changed files, such as one Cartouche Builder made after a change to nxnative ([builder/README.md](../builder/README.md)); one that names no commit is newer only by its version, so that a copy of the installed game written there later is never offered over it. The installed game's build is the one its IPA said when Cartouche installed it, kept in `Application Support/Cartouche/InstalledBuilds.json`, since the install changes the time of its `Info.plist`, with the version, commit and chain of the `Info.plist` in the game's bundle: not LiveContainer's settings for the game (`LCAppInfo.plist`, its `info()`), where Cartouche 0.9.0 to 0.11.2 looked for them and found none, so that their records take them from the bundle; for a game installed before 0.9.0, its install date stands for when it was built, since it was built before. The console then offers **Update** for that game, which installs the newer IPA over it as **Install from** does: its settings and saves stay. Of several IPAs of one game, the library takes the newest. An IPA nxnative built without its game files, a `romfs.id` in its app and no image, takes the image of them beside it, `romfs.img` in any letter case or a name ending so, of the size `romfs.id` names, else one of that size elsewhere in the games folder ([`CartoucheRomfs.swift`](Sources/CartoucheRomfs.swift), [decision 68](../docs/decisions.md)): **Install from** checks it against `romfs.id`, its size and the hash of its header and index, before it unpacks anything, copies it into the game's app after the IPA, where nxnative reads it, and checks the copy. A new build of the game keeps the image the installed one holds when it needs the same, and an IPA imported from Files takes the one beside it when Cartouche may read its folder. The storage page says which games lack their image, and which images no IPA there takes. **Install from** unpacks the IPA as it arrives ([`CartoucheZip.swift`](Sources/CartoucheZip.swift)), so the iPhone never holds the IPA and the game at once, after checking that the iPhone has room. A connection that drops on the way, when the Wi-Fi cuts out, the NAS restarts or iOS takes the connection of a Cartouche away from the screen, is made again for three minutes, and the install reads on from where it stopped, saying so meanwhile ([`CartoucheResumingSource.swift`](Sources/CartoucheResumingSource.swift)); an SMB request that gets no answer for a minute cuts its connection, which is then made again the same way, and a goodbye to a server that no longer answers waits five seconds at most. Installs and copies also keep going for the half-minute iOS gives an app that leaves the screen, and from iOS 26 until they are over ([decision 61](../docs/decisions.md), [`CartoucheContinuedWork.swift`](Sources/CartoucheContinuedWork.swift)): as the player starts one, Cartouche asks iOS to go on with it in the background, as a continued processing task, and iOS shows it in a Live Activity of its own, on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, with how far it has gone and where the game comes from or goes to. Over the console, Cartouche says the player can leave once iOS has taken it, and to keep it open otherwise. The Live Activity's stop button stops the install or the copy, and so does iOS when it ends the task, since nothing tells the two apart: Cartouche then says that it stopped outside, without a report. An install whose game is being signed, so in place already, finishes, and so does a step the player came back to. Unpacking an IPA from Files stops between two blocks of a file ([`livecontainer/0009`](../app/patches/README.md)), and a copy that stops removes what it wrote. An IPA from Files that ends early, or one of whose files changed since it was made, as when a copy stopped or went wrong, is refused as damaged, with nothing installed ([`livecontainer/0011`](../app/patches/README.md)), as **Install from** refuses one from the games folder; so is an IPA without its app in a `Payload` folder, or whose app's `Info.plist` cannot be read or does not give its bundle identifier. None of it takes more than a free Apple account: Cartouche's `Info.plist` lists the tasks' identifiers under its bundle identifier ([`app/scripts/package.sh`](../app/scripts/package.sh)), and the Cartouche Installer and the built-in SideStore move them under the one signing gives, as they do the bundle identifier. **Copy to** writes an installed game there as an IPA, after checking that the place has room. A games folder the player's account can only read still fills the library, but offers no **Copy to**, and **Uninstall** no copy first.
  - *Saves folder:* each game's saves are kept in step with it, both ways, as nxnative keeps a save in step with a cloud folder (`dm/host/save_sync.c`) ([decision 51](../docs/decisions.md), [`CartoucheSaveSync.swift`](Sources/CartoucheSaveSync.swift), [`CartoucheStorageSaves.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageSaves.swift), [`CartoucheSaveBackups.swift`](Sources/CartoucheSaveBackups.swift)). A copy is a zip of the game's `Documents/save` and `Documents/save_device`, the folders nxnative keeps its saves in, as `<game> [<title ID>]/<date>.zip`, named by when its saves were last written, in UTC, so that the newest is the save played last, whichever iPhone made it; one sent as the newest is named a second after the newest there, whatever the iPhone's clock says. The game's folder is found by its title ID, whatever name is before it, as a Mac's Cartouche Builder names it after the game's app for the Mac; of two folders of one game, made before either was seen, the one holding the newest copy is the game's, for every iPhone and the Mac alike. Two saves are the same when their files' paths, sizes and CRC-32s are, which a copy's central directory lists, read from its end without the rest. For each game, Cartouche keeps the base, the copy last known to be the same as the saves on the iPhone and how those looked then, and goes the way nxnative's `ss_decide` does: no saves on the iPhone, the newest copy comes; only the folder's changed, its newest copy comes; only the iPhone's changed, they go; both changed, or a first sync with saves on both sides, the saves written last win and the others are kept there as a copy of their own date. The newest copy comes when the game is installed, as after Cartouche was installed again, and before **Play** starts it, as after another iPhone played it; the game gets its data folder for it, as on its first start. The saves that changed go when a game behind the console ends, and, since a game in Cartouche's place is Cartouche itself while it plays, when Cartouche opens again after a game and whenever it comes back to the front, but for the game that runs. A copy that comes is downloaded and checked whole before it replaces the saves, which are set aside until it is in place; nxnative's working copy, `save.work`, goes, for the game to make it again. After each copy, the ten newest stay, the one just made among them. The game's sheet says what came and what was kept, syncs now, lists the copies and puts one back, after sending the saves it replaces; the copy put back then goes as the newest, which the player's other iPhones take at their next start. A server offline from here is left alone: the game plays with the saves it has, and those to send wait for it. It has to be a folder the player's account can write to: one it can only read is not offered.
  - *Read-only places:* whether the account can write is asked, never tried: the permissions of a shared folder come with the connection to it (SMB's `MaximalAccess`), and a folder's are asked by opening it to add a file and a folder in it, which adds neither ([`CartoucheStorageSMB.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageSMB.swift), `canWrite`); a folder in Files answers as its file system says. Cartouche asks when a server is saved, when the player looks through its folders, when it reads the games folder and before each copy, and keeps the answers, so that a shared folder the account can only read shows *read-only* and cannot hold the saves.
  - *Offline:* a server that does not answer from where the iPhone is, as a NAS at home away from home or off the player's VPN, is offline ([`CartoucheStorageReach.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageReach.swift)): the protocol's first connection to it waits six seconds at most, where TCP would wait over a minute before giving up, and an S3 server is knocked on before the first request, unless the iPhone goes through a proxy. Cartouche says so on the home's top bar, under the game in focus when its IPA or a newer build of it is there, on its sheet, on the System page and on the Storage page, which tries it again. The games installed play as ever, the games folder keeps its last list, and the copies of the saves wait, each game's sheet saying so; **Install from**, **Update** and **Copy to** say the server is offline and start nothing. What Cartouche does by itself each time it comes back leaves a server found offline alone for a minute, as long as the iPhone stays on the same network, so that **Play** never waits for it. It is tried again two seconds after each change of the iPhone's network, every minute while Cartouche is in front, and whenever the player asks, with a connection opened and closed at once, without a word to the server; once it answers, the games folder is read again and the saves waiting are copied. Being offline is not a problem: no report is made of it, nothing is counted as an error, and only the journal notes it. In a server's form, a server that does not answer says to check its address.
  - *S3:* a private bucket of the player's, on Amazon S3 or on a server of their own such as MinIO on a NAS, with an access key of their account ([`CartoucheStorageS3.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageS3.swift)). Every request is signed with the player's keys (AWS Signature Version 4, [`CartoucheS3.swift`](Sources/CartoucheS3.swift), tested against AWS's own examples), and none goes without them: there is no way to reach a public bucket. The address goes over HTTPS unless the player types `http://` for a server on their network; the region, when left empty, is the one the address names, else `us-east-1`, and Cartouche signs for the bucket's own when S3 says it is another. Amazon's buckets are named in the host, others in the path. "Folders" are keys up to a `/`, as S3 consoles show them. A game reads with ranged requests, 4 MiB at least at a time; a copy goes in one request up to 16 MiB, in parts otherwise, which S3 assembles only at the end, so that the file there stays until then. S3 cannot be asked whether keys may write: Cartouche writes a hidden file and removes it. The bucket is saved only once it lists for the keys.
  - *FTP:* a server of the player's network, such as their NAS's, with their account on it ([`CartoucheStorageFTP.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageFTP.swift)): Cartouche's own client on Network.framework, with no dependency. Data connections are passive (EPSV, else PASV), always to the server Cartouche signed in to, whatever address PASV names. Folders are listed with MLSD when the server has it, as its FEAT says by naming MLST (RFC 3659), else LIST as Unix and Windows servers write it ([`CartoucheFTP.swift`](Sources/CartoucheFTP.swift)). Listing and folders share one control connection, a command at a time; each file read or written gets one of its own. A game reads from an offset (REST, then RETR), 4 MiB at least at a time; a copy is written as `<name>.partial` and renamed once finished. FTP sends the password unencrypted, so the form says it is for the player's own network; the server is saved only once the account gets in, and an empty user name is refused.
  - *SFTP:* a server of the player's, such as their NAS's or their Mac's, with their account on it ([`CartoucheStorageSFTP.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageSFTP.swift)): the SSH connection is Apple's swift-nio-ssh, linked by [`0007`](../app/patches/livecontainer/0007-link-the-swift-nio-ssh-package.patch), and SFTP version 3 on it is Cartouche's own ([`CartoucheSFTP.swift`](Sources/CartoucheSFTP.swift)). The server's key (ed25519 or ECDSA, as swift-nio-ssh takes) is kept with the server when the player saves it, its fingerprint shown in the form; at each connection a server showing another key is refused before the password is sent, until the player saves the server again. Sign-in is by password only. Paths are from the folder the server opens the account in. Reads and writes go in 32 KiB requests, sixteen at a time on one channel; a game reads 4 MiB at least at a time, and a copy is written as `<name>.partial`, then put in place of the file there, if any. Whether the account can write is asked by making an empty hidden file and removing it. Builds without the package, such as the gallery, leave SFTP out.
  - *Google Drive:* the player's My Drive, with their own Google account, through an OAuth client of their own, of the iOS type, which they make once in their Google Cloud project ([decision 76](../docs/decisions.md), [`CartoucheStorageDrive.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageDrive.swift), [`CartoucheDrive.swift`](Sources/CartoucheDrive.swift), [`CartoucheStorageDriveView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageDriveView.swift)): Cartouche carries no client, key or secret of Google's. **Add Google Drive** takes the client's ID, or all the text Google Cloud gives with it, and **Make your client, step by step** opens each page of Google Cloud's console in turn, with Cartouche's bundle identifier to copy: the project, the Drive API, Google's sign-in page, the player as its test user, the client. The sign-in is Google's own page, in the sheet iOS shows for it (ASWebAuthenticationSession), with PKCE and no secret, back to the client's own scheme (`com.googleusercontent.apps.<client>:/oauth2redirect`), for the whole Drive (`auth/drive`): a games folder the player made in Drive, or one that Google Drive for desktop keeps in step with a folder of their Mac, such as Cartouche Builder's, is reached by no narrower scope. The refresh token stays in the keychain as the server's secret; an access token lasts an hour, and another is asked for with it. Google ends the sign-in of a project in testing after seven days: the Drive is then out of reach as a server away from home is, without a report and without a word more to Google, until the player signs in again, from **Sign in to Google again** on its Storage page, which the home screen's capsule, a game's sheet and the System page lead to. Paths are names from My Drive's root, composed or as a Mac decomposed them; of the files or folders of one name, which Drive allows, the newest; Google's own files, shortcuts, the bin and a name with a `/` are left out. Reads go by ranges, as for S3; a copy goes with Google's resumable upload, in pieces of 8 MiB, a piece that fails going on from where Google holds the file, which takes the place of the one there only once whole. Files are removed for good, the bin keeping them and their room for 30 days otherwise. Whether the account can write a folder is its `canAddChildren` capability; the room left is the account's quota. Google failing for a moment, or limiting the requests, is asked again after 0.5, 1, 2, 4 and 8 seconds. No redirection is followed, so that the access token goes to the Drive API alone. The storage tests run it against a stand-in for Google's servers ([`StorageTests`](StorageTests/README.md)).
  - *Your Mac:* Cartouche Builder, with **Share with Cartouche on the iPhone** turned on ([`CartoucheStorageMac.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageMac.swift), [`CartoucheStorageMacView.swift`](Sources/CartoucheStorageMacView.swift), [builder/README.md](../builder/README.md#the-iphone)). **Add your Mac** lists the Macs that share on this network (Bonjour, `_cartouche._tcp`, whose TXT record holds each Mac's ID) and pairs with the one the player picks: both screens show six digits made from the keys the two sides traded (X25519), and the player clicks **Allow** on the Mac when they are the same. The key they then share is the server's secret in this iPhone's keychain and never crosses the network: each session proves it on both sides, and every message after is encrypted with AES-GCM ([`CartoucheBridge.swift`](Sources/CartoucheBridge.swift), the same file in Cartouche Builder). The Mac shares two folders: **Games**, its games folder wherever it is, and **Saves**, the folder the player chose there for the copies; for a new Mac, they become the games folder and the saves folder when there are none yet. Cartouche reaches the Mac first at the address it last reached it at, through a VPN too, then by Bonjour; a Mac that answers neither within a few seconds is offline, as a NAS out of reach is (*Offline*, above), and is tried again the same two ways. Reads go ahead of a game's install in 4 MiB pieces, as S3's do, and the Mac writes a copy to a hidden file it names once whole. A Mac that removed this iPhone, or another Mac at the old address, asks to be paired again. [`StorageTests`](StorageTests/README.md) pairs with a fake Mac on the loopback and copies both ways.

## Every iPhone, the iPhone Duo included

The launcher lays itself out from the space it is given, never from the iPhone model, as Apple asks of apps for the iPhone Duo ([Preparing your app for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/preparing-your-app-for-iphone-duo)). One design then serves every iPhone, both displays of the Duo and the iPad. The model matters only to the game sheets, for memory.

| Where | Width class | Home |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Any other iPhone, portrait | compact | One column: the player's games, then the game in focus, in the middle, and the dock. A game's sheet opens over the home. |
| Any iPhone, landscape | compact | The shelf beside the game in focus. |
| iPhone Duo folded: outer display, 5.4 in | compact | As on the other iPhones. iOS moves the bars to the side of the display. |
| iPhone Duo unfolded: inner display, 7.6 in | regular | The home on one side of the fold, its shelf only, and the sheet of the game in focus on the other: side by side when the display is wider than tall, the home above when it is taller. Before the player has a game, the whole display, as on the other iPhones. |
| iPad | regular | As on the unfolded Duo. |

- **Size classes and our own bounds, not the screen.** Layout follows `horizontalSizeClass` and the bounds of the scene or the containing view, never `UIScreen.main.bounds`, `userInterfaceIdiom` or the interface orientation.
- **System containers and bars.** `NavigationStack`, `toolbar(content:)` and the bars iOS provides, which it presents vertically on the side of the Duo's outer display, and for some views on the inner one. Every bar item has an icon and a title: iOS does not present a title-only item or a custom view vertically. The banner of the player's games extends under a vertical bar with `backgroundExtensionEffect()`.
- **Regular width.** A split `ArrangementView` (iOS 27.1) holds the home and the sheet of the game in focus, the same size each, and iOS places them around the fold: side by side, the home leading, when the display is wider than tall, the home above when it is taller, and it moves them as the Duo folds partly ([`CartoucheArrangement.swift`](Sources/CartoucheArrangement.swift)). It sits at the top level, not inside a split view, list or scroll view, as Apple advises. The home then leaves out the game in focus, whose sheet is beside it, and its shelf takes its whole width. The sheet follows the focus, by touch as with a controller, and opens the game's settings in its place; with a controller, B closes them and L and R change their section. On the cover that imports a game, or the one for every game, and in All games until another game is chosen there, the sheet of the last game in focus stays. Before the player has a game of their own, nothing stands beside the console, never a game they do not have: the arrangement has no axis, as in a compact width, and the home, with the game in focus under its shelf, and All games take the whole display, as on an iPhone, a game's sheet opening over them. With the player's first game, an open sheet moves beside them. Before iOS 27.1, the sheet opens as a card over the home.
- **Nothing in the fold or under a camera.** Views we draw ourselves read `reservedRegions(kind:options:layoutDirectionBehavior:)` and keep text and controls out of the fold while the Duo is partly folded, and out of the outer front camera, which always covers part of the outer display. System views move on their own.
- **Folding loses nothing.** Folding, unfolding or rotating keeps the selected game, the scroll position and any import in progress; imports run outside the views. The same `ArrangementView` stays on screen in every width, with no axis to split along in a compact one, so that the home is never built again: unfolding moves an open sheet beside the home, and folding brings open game settings back over it, in a sheet.
- **Built with Xcode 27.2.** Apple: an app built with Xcode 26 or earlier does not extend under the Duo's status bar and camera; on a Duo, 0.1 showed in a narrower band beside a black one that held the status bar. Since 0.2, CI builds Cartouche with Xcode 27.2 and the iOS 27.2 SDK on GitHub's `xcode-27` runners.
- **Tested** in the Duo simulator, by the gallery on every pull request that changes the screens, then on a Duo closed, open and partly folded, rotated in each pose, as Apple recommends.

### Games on the Duo

A game's picture and its touch controls are drawn by nxnative's iOS app, which runs inside Cartouche or, behind the console, in a process of its own whose scene fills a window of Cartouche's, and Cartouche does not patch games. Inside Cartouche, system libraries work to the SDK version Cartouche was built with, not the game's, unless LiveContainer's per-app *Spoof SDK Version* setting is on ([`Dyld.m`](https://github.com/LiveContainer/LiveContainer/blob/9108ae624ea001f7d7696d48407f890b48fa2653/LiveContainer/Tweaks/Dyld.m)): built with Xcode 27.2, Cartouche lets games use the whole display, the camera area included. nxnative's iOS app lays its picture and touch controls out again whenever its view changes size, from the view's bounds, its safe area and the fold (`UIHingeInteraction`, iOS 27.1; `dm/ios/Sources/DmLayout.h` in nxnative): the controls over the picture when the view is wider than tall, unless the Duo is partly open over a horizontal fold, and otherwise the picture above the fold and the controls below. Each layout sizes the picture's drawable to the view, at the display's scale, and lets go of the buttons held, so that a player who folds the Duo mid-race presses them again. Still to check on a Duo: folding or unfolding mid-game neither restarts the game nor pushes it over its memory limit. Behind the console, the game's scene takes the bounds of Cartouche's window and follows them as the Duo folds or turns, and the game's process records each size it was given with its footprint (`sizes` in `Cartouche/GameMemory.plist`); still to check there: the fold as `UIHingeInteraction` reports it inside the game's scene, the reserved regions of the outer camera and of the fold, and the Home button at the bottom of the game's screen, which the views Cartouche draws over the game place by the safe area only.
