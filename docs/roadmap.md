# Roadmap

Cartouche is a launcher: it installs, starts and keeps the games the player builds with nxnative, and signs, renews and updates itself. Today the app does not build games ([decision 28](decisions.md)): Cartouche Builder, a program of its own, builds them on the player's Mac ([decision 29](decisions.md)). Building on the iPhone or on a Windows PC, playing on the Mac, and installing Cartouche with no computer, signed with the player's own certificate, are planned ([decisions 55, 60 and 79](decisions.md)). Each release is checked on a real iPhone, not only in CI, and tested from a fresh install.

## Later

- **Building on the iPhone, or on a Windows PC.** A game built from the player's own files on the iPhone itself, with no Mac: Cartouche reads the game's package and the console's keys from Files or the player's own storage, runs nxnative's build on the phone and installs the game. What it takes: nxnative's code rewrite on iOS, which spike S2 ran on an iPhone in 0.1 with the very result of the Mac; the shaders, and the parts a Mac compiles with Xcode today; and room on the phone, in memory and in time, for the largest games ([decision 55](decisions.md)). The same pieces let Cartouche Builder build on a Windows PC too: the parts compiled with Xcode come ready-made, compiled on a Mac by the project with no game in them, since Apple's licence keeps its SDKs to Apple computers; the shaders are compiled with Apple's Metal tools for Windows, once nxnative's build takes a Metal compiler other than Xcode 27.2's; and a game that nxnative starts on a Mac during its build, to take its shaders out (Fire Emblem: Three Houses, the Unreal games), still needs a Mac ([decision 60](decisions.md)).
- **Playing on the Mac.** The Mac app plays the player's games too: Cartouche Builder builds each game for the Mac as well, as nxnative plays it there, starts it with a controller, and keeps its saves in step with the iPhone's through the saves folder, which can be on that very Mac: a game left on the iPhone goes on on the Mac, and back ([decision 55](decisions.md)).
- **Installing Cartouche with no computer, with the player's own certificate.** A Cartouche IPA of its own, published with each release, that the player signs on the iPhone itself with their own signing certificate (`.p12`) and its provisioning profile. At its first start, Cartouche asks for that same certificate, its password and its profile, keeps them in the iPhone's keychain and signs the games with them: no computer, no Apple ID, no LocalDevVPN and no renewal each week. In exchange, games start in Cartouche's place, an update is the new IPA signed again, which Cartouche only says is out, and a revoked certificate stops everything until Cartouche is signed again. What it takes: the Cartouche Installer's option for the player's own certificate, first, which brings that mode ([imported signing](imported-signing.md)); an IPA already set up that way, with no app extensions and no app group; Cartouche reading its own profile and saying itself when it lacks Increased Memory Limit, without which most games do not start, or the keychain groups the games need, since no installer is there to check them; and signing tools that keep the profile's entitlements. Nothing is begun, and no such certificate has been tried yet ([decision 79](decisions.md)).

## Not planned

- Hosting, downloading or listing downloadable games, keys or firmware ([decision 1](decisions.md)).
